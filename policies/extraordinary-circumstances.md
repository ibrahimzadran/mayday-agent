# Extraordinary Circumstances

When a disruption removes the airline's obligation to pay statutory
compensation under EU261 or UK261.

## EXT-1 The test

EXT-1.1 A circumstance is extraordinary when it is **not inherent in the
normal exercise of the airline's activity** and is **beyond its actual
control**.

EXT-1.2 The airline must also show it took all reasonable measures to avoid
the disruption.

## EXT-2 Usually extraordinary

- Severe weather at the origin, destination, or en route
- Air traffic control restrictions and flow programmes
- Airport or airspace closure
- Security alerts and threats
- Political instability
- Bird strike
- Strikes by third parties, such as airport or ATC staff
- A passenger medical emergency forcing a diversion

## EXT-3 Usually NOT extraordinary

- **Technical and mechanical faults discovered during routine operation.**
  These are inherent in running an airline, even when unexpected. Compensation
  is normally owed.
- Crew shortages and crew scheduling failures
- Strikes by the airline's own staff
- Late arrival of the inbound aircraft, unless the original cause was itself
  extraordinary
- IT and systems failures
- Overbooking

## EXT-4 Applying it

EXT-4.1 The recorded reason for the disruption determines the answer. Agents
must use the reason on the flight record and must not reinterpret it.

EXT-4.2 Extraordinary circumstances remove **compensation only**. The right to
rebooking, refund, and care under EU261-5 always survives.
