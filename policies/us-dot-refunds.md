# US DOT Refund Rules

Summary of US Department of Transportation requirements for flights to, from
and within the United States.

## US-DOT-1 When a refund is owed

US-DOT-1.1 A passenger is entitled to a refund when their flight is
**cancelled**, or **significantly changed**, and they decline the alternative
offered.

US-DOT-1.2 A change is significant when it results in:

- departure or arrival more than **3 hours** later for a domestic flight
- departure or arrival more than **6 hours** later for an international flight
- a downgrade to a lower class of service
- a change of departure or arrival airport
- an increase in the number of connections

## US-DOT-2 Form of the refund

US-DOT-2.1 Refunds are automatic. The passenger does not have to ask.

US-DOT-2.2 Refunds are made in the **original form of payment**, not as
vouchers or credit, unless the passenger affirmatively chooses a voucher.

US-DOT-2.3 Credit card refunds are processed within 7 business days. Other
payment methods within 20 calendar days.

US-DOT-2.4 A non-refundable fare is still refundable under this rule. The fare
rules do not override it. See FR-3.3.

## US-DOT-3 What US rules do NOT require

US-DOT-3.1 There is **no US equivalent of EU261 cash compensation**. A
passenger on a purely domestic US itinerary is owed a refund or rebooking, but
not a compensation payment, no matter how long the delay.

US-DOT-3.2 Meals and hotels are not federally required in the US. Meridian
provides them as a matter of its own policy. See VOU-1 and VOU-3.

## US-DOT-4 Baggage

US-DOT-4.1 Checked bag fees are refunded if a bag is delayed more than 12
hours on a domestic flight, or 15 to 30 hours on an international flight
depending on length.
