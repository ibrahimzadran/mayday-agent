# Cancellation Policy

## CX-1 Controllable versus uncontrollable

CX-1.1 A cancellation is **controllable** when it results from something
within Meridian's control. Examples: crew availability, crew scheduling,
aircraft maintenance, IT systems, fuelling, catering, baggage handling.

CX-1.2 A cancellation is **uncontrollable** when it results from something
outside Meridian's control. Examples: severe weather, air traffic control
restrictions, security incidents, airport closure, bird strike, medical
diversion, national strikes not involving Meridian staff.

CX-1.3 The distinction determines what the passenger receives. It does not
change their right to rebooking or refund, which apply either way.

## CX-2 What the passenger gets

| | Controllable | Uncontrollable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rebooking at no charge | Yes | Yes |
| Refund if they decline rebooking | Yes | Yes |
| Meal voucher | Yes | Yes |
| Hotel for an overnight disruption | Yes | No, offered at distressed rates |
| Statutory compensation | Depends on EU261 / UK261 | Usually none, see EXT-1 |

## CX-3 Notification

CX-3.1 Passengers are notified by SMS and email as soon as a cancellation is
confirmed.

CX-3.2 The reason given to the passenger must match the operational reason
recorded for the flight. Agents must not speculate about causes.
